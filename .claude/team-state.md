Session 11 additions (2026-07-16):

- **Product Decision Records (PDRs) — the fourth layer of why.** The repo governed three: why each rule exists (DoD callouts), why the code is shaped this way (ADRs), why the consultancy exists (`gtm/`, never synced). Nothing recorded why the *software* exists. The gap was structural, not an oversight: every audit domain compares one repo artifact to another, and purpose has no counterpart in the repo, so there was nothing to compare against. The audit could prove code drifted from docs; it could never prove the product drifted from its reason for existing.
- **Why it mattered for the ICP:** non-technical founders. The contractor holds the code, the founder holds the thesis. Governance covered everything the contractor holds and nothing the founder holds — the one asset the buyer personally owns was the one the system didn't protect. `gtm/one-pager.md` already sells "a record of what was built and why".
- **The load-bearing rule:** *a decision without a falsifier is a wish.* Every PDR ships with the observable condition that would retire it and cannot reach Accepted without one — the product-layer form of "enforcement ships with the promise". A PDR without a falsifier is a vision statement, and vision statements don't rot loudly.
- **Design decisions:** PDR corpus (not a standing doc) so it reuses numbering, index, supersession, and the ADR audit domain. Non-goals are records with their own numbers, not sections — highest-signal artifact, since the audit can check shipped work against a stated non-goal and can do that for nothing else at this layer. Traceability at feature/epic level only, with `Serves: none` always legitimate.
- **Sync firewall (the highest-stakes piece):** `/sync-from-repo` reads live client repos and abstracts into templates that ship to *other clients*. PDR content is a client's market thesis — frequently confidential, and identifying even anonymized, because a good PDR is specific enough to be wrong. **The shape syncs; the records never do.** Harder line than `gtm/`: that's propose-don't-apply, this is don't-propose. Same for `docs/watch-items/` bodies.
- **`/review-sync` must never generate a PDR bootstrap prompt.** A prompt is what an agent runs alone, and an agent alone will invent a thesis or write a mission statement. A fabricated PDR is worse than an empty directory — it gets cited in review as though someone believed it. Prompts may install the form, index, ADR-023, and the lint, then say "book the interview".
- **`pdr-interview` skill — a new skill shape.** Every other skill discovers by probing, which works because architecture is in the codebase. Purpose isn't. But blank-slate asking produces mush, so: probe first, draft candidates from cited evidence, surface the repo's self-contradictions, *then* ask. The contradictions are the interview — they're the one question a human can't answer with a platitude.
- **Doctrine inversion, recorded in `sync-from-repo` Step 4:** these templates were authored *before* the source repo had them, inverting the normal harvest flow. They ship as **candidates**; the 3-cycle clock starts at ai-fleet adoption. Until then treat them as *less* authoritative than harvested templates — they've never been stress-tested against a real incident.

## Shared Gotchas

- DORA mapping section removed from founder-ICP one-pager — not the buyer's vocabulary.
- "Launch window coverage" engagement shape is new and not in the CTO one-pager — formalized from BModelr pattern.
- **Closing keywords near an issue reference auto-close it — anywhere in the commit message, including inside quoted text.** GitHub treats `fix`/`fixes`/`close`/`closes`/`resolve`/`resolves` as closing keywords, and a conventional-commit colon does not break the match. Happened twice on 2026-07-24, both times with the work unfinished: `0e328e9` had the subject `fix: #1 escalated to frontier`, and then `0761111` — **the commit recording this very gotcha** — closed #1 again because its body quoted that subject as an example. Documenting the hazard reproduced it. **Rules:** never start a subject with `<type>: #N`; put references later (`fix: escalate #1 to frontier`) or in the body; and when quoting an offending message, mask the reference (`fix: #<N>` or `issue 1`) rather than reproducing it verbatim.
- **Git push auth (leizerowicz repos):** The `gh-real` credential helper reads `~/.config/gh/hosts.yml`, where the default account is `greghopskip` (no write access to `leizerowicz/*` repos). `gh auth switch` doesn't help when `GH_TOKEN` env is unset but the helper still picks the wrong account. **Fix:** push with the token inline: `TOKEN=$(gh auth token) && git push https://gleizerowicz:${TOKEN}@github.com/leizerowicz/<repo>.git <branch>`. The `gh auth token` command returns the active account's token (gleizerowicz when that's the active `gh` account).

Session 16 additions (2026-07-26):

- **Measured the tiering practice against three live backlogs and found the ratio problem is decomposition, not rubric.** Readings: ai-fleet 63% escalated (10/16 tiered open), analytics-infrastructure 85% (23/27), repo-governance 83% (5/6, all-state). Aggregate across all states: 76% above-standard, 54 tiered. Target discussed was 10%; **set to 20% as the working target** since the practice is unproven, with a per-repo ramp toward 10% as repos mature.
- **The diagnosis.** Issues are scoped by *component*; tiers are assigned by *highest signal present*. Those two rules multiply — any component touching a boundary anywhere inherits the whole boundary, so at component granularity 76% is the arithmetically correct answer. The rubric is fine; the unit of work is wrong. analytics-infrastructure #230–#259 is one epic, 22 issues, every title `Gateway: <component>`, ~7 of them on a single entitlement predicate.
- **The triagers wrote the confession into the tier lines.** #238 "**mostly mechanical delivery, but** the principal-keyed result cache is a silent cross-tenant leak surface"; #1186 "(The starter-prompt catalog alone would be **standard; highest signal wins**.)"; #1280 "**mostly built** — residual is converging the two scoring entry points." Each is a correct application of highest-signal-wins *and* a missed split.
- **Split was documented but never operationalized — 2 splits in 38 escalations.** Compare instrumentation: *downgrade* had a hard rule, an anti-gaming argument, lint R6 with GraphQL `userContentEdits`, and audit signal 3. *Split* had one paragraph and one bullet in the human interview, and wasn't in the classifier's required outputs at all. The policy said "prefer the split" and then never asked for one.

**Shipped (policy v1.8.0):**

- **The decomposition rule — decompose first, tier the residue.** No issue is tiered above `standard` until decomposition is attempted and *on the record*: either a split proposal (N standard children + residue) or a **non-splittability statement** naming the mechanism ("a single transaction the change must land inside", "one function whose every branch composes the predicate"). "It's all one thing" is not a statement. Same move the `kind` field made one level up — forcing a declaration converts a default into a decision, and this one is checkable against the merged diff afterwards.
- **Tier-line format extended.** Every escalation now carries `Not splittable: <mechanism>` or `Split from|into #NNN`. Malformed without it, same as a missing kind.
- **Frontier *ratio* separated from inherent *population*.** The ratio is a decomposition metric (should trend down, moves by splitting); the population is a risk metric (should not trend). Splitting doesn't reduce risk, it isolates it — that's the intended behavior, not gaming. New derived signal: **decomposition debt = escalations ÷ distinct surfaces they name.** Anti-pattern 8 rewritten (it read as an argument against any ratio target); 9 and 10 added (tiering before decomposing; scoping issues by component).
- **Per-repo target ramp** in `downstream/hopskip/_client.md`: bootstrap = record a baseline and target nothing; adopting ≤ 20%; mature ≤ 10%. **20% is explicitly provisional and carries its own falsifier** — a repo stalling at 25% with defensible `Not splittable:` sentences on every escalation is evidence the target was wrong, and that gets recorded. analytics-infrastructure held at *bootstrap* despite the higher number because ~22 of its 23 escalations are one component-scoped epic.
- **R7 in `check-issue-routing.mjs` (v1.1.0) — the mechanical-majority tell as a lint.** Flags hedge phrases in an escalated issue's tier line, **clears when a decomposition record is present**. That interlock is the design: the cheapest way to silence the lint is to do what the policy asks. Verified live — fires on exactly #238, #1186, #1280, zero false positives across 33 escalations. Also prints a decomposition census (escalations, split, declared, undeclared) feeding audit signal 5.
- **Classifier agents (both harnesses, v1.1.0):** "Split candidates" → "Decomposition — required for every escalation." New required `Decomposition` column in the proposal table; a row above `standard` with it empty is incomplete. Explicit instruction to catch the hedge in its *own* prose before the lint does.
- **Skill v1.4.0:** interview reordered so **splits are asked first** (they change which issues are up for discussion at all); non-splittability statements are read back with the question "is this mechanism real?", not "do you agree this is hard"; Step 3 creates split children *before* tiering parents (parent tier lines reference child numbers); Step 6 reports ratio-against-target, decomposition debt, and splits proposed/accepted/declined.

Explicit warning **not** to bulk-append `Not splittable:` to clear the lint — that converts a finding into a rubber stamp, the same flattering-call failure one level up. Parents of already-split issues need their tier lines edited to say so; the `standard` children are not the record.
